About the meeting

This five-day meeting at ESA/ESTEC, organised by the NewAthena Science Working Group 2 (SWG2), brings together researchers working on galaxies and supermassive black holes in X-rays. The meeting is science-driven: centred on the presentation and discussion of results spanning the full scope of SWG2 science.

The timing is deliberate. Scheduled after the SWG2 Special Issue deadline in the Journal of High Energy Astrophysics, the meeting provides a natural forum for authors to present their recently submitted work. The meeting is open to all SWG2 science, not only to researchers contributing to the Special Issue — anyone working in the field is warmly encouraged to present their work.

NewAthena & SWG2

NewAthena (planned launch: late 2030s) is ESA's next flagship X-ray observatory. SWG2 — Galaxies and Supermassive Black Holes — gathers researchers working on AGN physics, galaxy evolution, and the cosmic X-ray background.

Scientific Scope

NewAthena SWG2 encompasses studies of galaxies and their AGN, and their coevolution at all scales: the geometry and origin of X-ray emitting regions, AGN outflows and their energetics, AGN and stellar feedback on host galaxies, and AGN population studies up to high redshift to constrain the seeds and growth of supermassive black holes.

Francisco J Carrera Instituto de Fisica de Cantabria (CSIC-Universidad de Cantabria)
Tracing the Cosmic Accretion History with NewAthena: From X-ray Binaries to Supermassive Black Holes
Abstract

The NewAthena mission will provide an unprecedented view of cosmic accretion, from stellar-mass X-ray binaries in galaxies to supermassive black holes (SMBHs) at the epoch of reionization. We present a unified forward-modeling framework to assess the ability of Wide Field Imager (WFI) surveys to recover the populations and physical properties of accreting sources and their host galaxies.

Using realistic mock catalogs of AGN and galaxies combined with WFI survey specifications, we simulate X-ray populations and measured properties. The framework incorporates positional and photometric uncertainties, counterpart identification, and SED fitting. This enables a quantitative evaluation of the recovery and biases of stellar mass, star-formation rate, X-ray luminosity, and specific accretion rate. We assess the robustness of these measurements and identify complementary observations that can improve them.

We further evaluate NewAthena’s capability to constrain the cosmic evolution of X-ray binaries and SMBH growth through specific accretion-rate distributions and forward modeling. This will provide a comprehensive assessment of the WFI surveys’ potential to trace accretion power across the compact-object mass spectrum, with important implications for galaxy-black hole co-evolution, feedback processes, and gravitational-wave sources from stellar-remnant and SMBH mergers.

Alex McNamara ESA
Straylight effect on WFI
Abstract

Stray light from bright X-ray sources outside the nominal field of view introduce background features in NewAthena Wide Field Imager (WFI) observations, affecting source detection and scientific performance. This work investigates the impact of different optical configurations on stray-light performance, with the aim of identifying optical designs that minimise the stray light effect on WFI observations. Stray light contributions are characterised using SIXTE-simulated observations for multiple optical configurations. Source detection is then performed, and the detected sources are cross-correlated with the input source catalogues. This enables systematic assessment of source detectability and distribution under different stray-light configurations, supporting optimisation of the optical design to minimise the impact of stray light on WFI observations.

Jialai Wang University of Science and Technology of China
X-ray Insights into Massive Black Holes in Dwarf Galaxies
Abstract

Dwarf galaxies offer a unique window into the origins and early growth of massive black holes (MBHs), potentially hosting relics of the seeds of today's supermassive black holes. However, establishing a census of MBHs in these low-mass systems remains challenging, as many may be weakly accreting or dormant. In this talk, I will discuss how X-ray observations can help address this challenge. First, I will discuss AT 2018cqh, a tidal disruption event in a dwarf galaxy whose unusual, long-lasting X-ray evolution is consistent with an intermediate-mass black hole interpretation. Second, I will present our Chandra study of X-ray binaries (XRBs) in dwarf galaxies from the Legacy ExtraGalactic UV Survey (LEGUS). By combining Chandra observations with Hubble Space Telescope imaging of resolved stellar populations, we construct X-ray luminosity functions for different XRB donor populations and investigate their contributions to the X-ray emission of dwarf galaxies. Together, these studies highlight different aspects of X-ray searches for massive black holes, from revealing potential black hole activity through transient events to characterizing stellar X-ray sources that may complicate the identification of faint AGNs. They also provide valuable insights for future NewAthena observations and constraining black hole populations in dwarf galaxies.

Luca Zappacosta INAF - Astronomical Observatory of Rome
Constraining Coronal Physics and Early SMBH Accretion in z > 6 Quasars with NewAthena
Abstract

Characterizing the nuclear properties of quasars (QSOs) powered by SMBHs with masses >10^9 M_sun during the Epoch of Reionization (EoR, z>6) is fundamental to understanding the efficient feeding and feedback mechanism driving early SMBH growth. Coronal X-ray emission crucially provides the most direct window into the innermost accretion process.

Based on two deep XMM-Newton programmes (~3 Ms) targeting 28 luminous QSOs at z=6-7.6, we revealed a distinct nuclear regime in the EoR: (i) X-ray spectra are significantly softer than at lower redshift (Gamma ~2.4) or feature low-energy (~30 keV) cut-offs, and (ii) spectral slopes correlate with broad-line wind velocities, linking coronal physics directly to accretion-driven disc outflows. These features point toward cold, thick coronae driven by super-Eddington accretion. Unambiguously disentangling these physical coronal parameters and linking them to the feedback-driving semi-relativistic winds requires coverage from next-generation X-ray observatories.

Here, we present NewAthena simulations showing how deep WFI and X-IFU observations on the brightest QSOs will constrain the key regime of early SMBH growth. WFI pointings will break continuum degeneracies, enabling accurate measurements of coronal temperature, optical depth, and cold reflection in the EoR. In synergy with WFI, deep X-IFU exposures will enable ultra-fast outflows detection at the earliest epochs, probing the coronal-wind connection in the semi-relativistic regime.

Nuno Covas Instituto de Astrofísica e Ciências do Espaço
Tracing the earliest and most obscured Super Massive Black Holes with NewAthena
Abstract

Understanding how super massive black holes formed and grew in the early Universe requires uncovering both the first accreting black holes and the heavily obscured populations missed by current surveys. NewAthena’s WFI will transform this landscape, probing AGN demographics from beyond Cosmic Noon to the Epoch of Reionization. We assess this potential through end-to-end SIXTE simulations based on the ASTRID cosmological hydrodynamical simulation, incorporating realistic backgrounds, source detection and Bayesian spectral analysis.

In the deepest survey tier, we predict 18 AGN detections at z > 6, including 2 at z > 7, extending the X-ray luminosity frontier by more than an order of magnitude compared with current surveys. The input X-ray luminosity function is recovered within 1σ across substantial luminosity ranges at 6 < z < 7.5, demonstrating WFI’s potential to constrain the abundance of early accreting black holes. Complementing this view of the first growing black holes, NewAthena will also unveil the heavily obscured population that remains elusive to current surveys. At 3 < z < 4, we predict 38 detected Compton-thick AGN, with up to 18 well characterized when multiwavelenght information is included. Together, these results highlight NewAthena’s ability to deliver unprecedented demographic constraints on both the earliest and the most heavily obscured growing supermassive black holes, shedding new light on their formation and evolution across cosmic time.

Darius Michienzi University of Bristol
Breaking coronal geometry degeneracies with relativistic reflection: from NuSTAR to NewAthena
Abstract

The geometry of the corona in active galactic nuclei (AGN) and X-ray binaries remains an open question, and determining the nature of this primary X-ray source is one of NewAthena’s driving science goals. Recent X-ray polarimetry points towards a corona extended parallel to the disc, but its exact shape remains unknown. Relativistic reflection offers a complementary probe, as the coronal geometry sets the disc emissivity profile imprinted on the reflection spectrum. We will present several models of coronal geometry fitted to NuSTAR observations of the X-ray binary MAXI J1348-630, whose almost unobscured view of the inner accretion flow makes it an ideal test case. These models use emissivity profiles, line profiles and reflection spectra generated by the relativistic ray-tracing code Gradus.jl. Since these calculations are mass-independent in units of gravitational radii, X-ray binaries provide high-S/N laboratories of the same accretion physics NewAthena will study across the AGN population. We use these fits to explore the degeneracies between corona models at NuSTAR resolution. High-resolution spectroscopy offers a way to break them: XRISM now separates the broad Fe K line from narrow emission and absorption features. Using simulated X-IFU spectra, with ~4 eV resolution and several times the Fe K effective area of XRISM/Resolve, we assess which of these geometries NewAthena can distinguish in bright AGN.

Niek Bollemeijer Dr. Remeis-Sternwarte Bamberg, FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg & ECAP
X-ray reflection spectra from extended coronae in accreting black holes
Abstract

Accreting black holes can be bright X-ray sources, with most of the emission originating from a Comptonizing region close to the black hole that is often called the corona. Part of the hard X-ray emission from the corona is reflected by the accretion disk, creating prominent features in the energy spectrum. The exact shape of this reflection spectrum encodes information about disk properties and is broadened by relativistic effects and interpretation requires detailed modelling. So far, most reflection modelling has been done with a lamppost approximation or a phenomenological emissivity prescription, limiting our ability to interpret results with confidence. Recently, important steps have been taken to model more realistic coronal geometries. I will present updates for the reflection model relxill, which allow a broad range of coronal geometries to be tested and fit to data and highlight how NewAthena's large effective area and spectral resolution will improve our ability to extract information from the reflection spectrum.

Javier Garcia NASA Goddard Space Flight Center
Advances in Relativistic Reflection Modeling for AGN with NewAthena
Abstract

NewAthena will transform X-ray studies of active galactic nuclei by combining a large collecting area with the high spectral resolution of the X-IFU, opening a regime in which the interpretation of AGN spectra will be limited as much by model fidelity as by photon statistics. In this talk, I will review recent developments in relativistic reflection models built on xillver that are aimed at meeting this challenge. These include high-density plasma effects, emission from the plunging region, returning radiation, an improved treatment of Compton scattering at microcalorimeter resolution, vertically extended disks, and transmission through partially ionized media. Using representative X-IFU simulations, I will illustrate how several of these effects can imprint measurable signatures in NewAthena spectra and assess their impact on inferred quantities such as black hole spin, disk structure, and coronal geometry. These advances highlight the need for physically complete and computationally robust reflection models to fully exploit NewAthena’s unprecedented spectral capabilities.

Peter Boorman Max Planck Institute for Extraterrestrial Physics
Unravelling the Supermassive Black Hole Spin Distribution with NewAthena
Abstract

Constraining the growth history of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) is key to understanding structure formation across cosmic time. Different growth mechanisms are predicted to leave distinct imprints on SMBH spin distributions, making population-level spin measurements a potential probe of how SMBHs grow. Relativistic reprocessing of coronal X-rays by the accretion flow in active galactic nuclei (AGN) can provide spin measurements, making this a compelling goal for future NewAthena extragalactic surveys. However, X-ray spectra for much of the AGN population may not constrain spin accurately, particularly when affected by ionised absorption, a soft excess, circum-nuclear obscuration, or a complete lack of relativistic reflection signatures altogether. In this talk, I will statistically explore the conditions under which SMBH spin can be measured in the NewAthena energy band using relativistic X-ray reflection spectral modelling. Using representative simulations for the NewAthena Wide Field Imager (WFI) and X-ray Integral Field Unit (X-IFU), I will forecast the mission’s ability to constrain the local SMBH spin distribution while accounting for spectral complexity that can obscure the relativistic reflection signal. Finally, using the HORIZON-AGN cosmological simulation as input, I will present an illustrative example for which NewAthena spin measurements could distinguish between growth through accretion alone and growth through both accretion and SMBH mergers.

Daniele Tagliacozzo INFN Roma 2 'Tor Vergata'
Decoding AGN coronae: NewAthena meets X-ray polarimetry
Abstract

The Imaging X-ray Polarimetry Explorer (IXPE) have opened a new window on the morphology of hot coronae in radio-quiet, unobscured AGN. However, interpreting the polarization signal depends critically on a reliable spectral decomposition and on accurate constraints on physical parameters such as the coronal temperature and black hole spin. In fact, IXPE observations and Monte Carlo simulations have shown that uncertainties in these parameters and in the relative contributions of direct, reflected and scattered emission can lead to different, sometimes conflicting, interpretations of the same polarization measurement. IXPE's observations of the Seyfert galaxy NGC 4151 provides a compelling example.

NewAthena's high-resolution spectroscopy will help disentangle these components and constrain the physical properties of the innermost regions of AGN. Using simulated NewAthena spectra and Monte Carlo Radiative Transfer models, I will explore how improved component separation and tighter spectral constraints translate into more robust estimates of the intrinsic coronal polarization and a narrower range of viable coronal geometries.

By reducing spectral ambiguities, NewAthena can make X-ray polarization a more reliable diagnostic of coronal physics and geometry, strengthening the interpretation of IXPE results and the scientific return of future polarimetric missions.

Alessia Tortosa INAF - OAS
Super-Eddington accreting AGN through the eyes of NewAthena
Abstract

Feeding beyond the limit set by their own radiation, super-Eddington AGN are nature’s most extreme accretors: radiation pressure inflates the disc, traps photons and launches powerful winds. They may have built the first SMBHs, yet their disc-corona geometry and winds remain poorly constrained. Our broad-band XMM-Newton/NuSTAR study of the SEAMBH sample (reverberation-mapped, 1 < λEdd < 426) reveals steep continua, low coronal temperatures, enhanced reflection and powerful ionised outflows. These local sources are analogues of high-z accretors, echoing the X-ray slope-UV wind link in luminous z > 6 quasars and the X-ray weakness of JWST selected AGN and Little Red Dots. Building on this, we present NewAthena/X-IFU simulations of five SEAMBHs from our contribution to the NewAthena Special Issue, submitted for internal revision. X-IFU will recover Γ to a few percent, detect coronal cutoffs of ~45-60 keV from continuum curvature alone, and measure the ionisation and column density of multiphase winds, from warm absorbers to UFOs, to <0.1 dex and their velocities to a few percent. It will also distinguish relativistic-disc from BLR origins of the broad Fe Kα line at >5σ in most sources, while the soft excess remains degenerate between warm Comptonisation and blurred reflection. We discuss how these measurements will constrain the high-λEdd branch of the V-shaped Γ-λEdd relation, linking super-Eddington disc-corona coupling and feedback to SMBH growth across cosmic time.

Kaushik Chatterjee SWIFAR, Yunnan University
A Unified Spectro-Temporal-Polarimetric View of the Complete 2025–2026 Outburst of GS 1354–64: From a Precessing Corona to a Stable Extended Slab
Abstract

We present a multi-satellite study of the black hole candidate GS 1354–64 during its 2025–2026 outburst, the first complete hard-to-soft-to-hard transition observed from this source. Using NuSTAR spectroscopy and timing together with IXPE polarimetry, we track the evolving accretion geometry. In the hard state, strong Type-C QPOs rise from 0.08 Hz to 1.73 Hz and anti-correlate with the inner disk radius, while a highly ionized Fe XXVI disk wind is detected. In the soft state, joint reflection modeling yields a near-maximal spin (a* ≈ 0.998), an inclination of i ≈ 51.5°, and an ISCO-pinned disk. Soft-state QPOs shift to 4.7–6.2 Hz and weaken. Simultaneous spectro-polarimetry shows an energy-increasing polarization degree, reaching about 7.5–9.1%, with a constant polarization angle, favoring a radially extended slab corona over spherical or lamppost geometries. These results provide a unified spectro-temporal-polarimetric picture of the outburst.

Kriti Kamal Gupta Leibniz-Institut für Astrophysik Potsdam (AIP)
Differential X-Ray Evolution of Optically Selected Ongoing Changing-Look AGN
Abstract

In recent years, optical photometric and spectroscopic wide-area surveys have revealed a population of extremely variable AGN, called changing-look AGN (CLAGN), in which the rest-frame UV-optical spectra show the (dis)appearance of the blue continuum and/or the broad emission lines. These changes can also be accompanied by changes in the X-ray flux and spectral shape. Over the past decade, hundreds of CLAGN have been discovered, but post-transition. Identifying these sources during transition and following them in the multi-wavelength is crucial to understand their underlying physical processes.

Using a combination of optical light curves from ZTF and repeated optical spectroscopy from SDSS-V, we identified two CLAGN in transition. Both sources were caught during transition thanks to a sudden increase in their optical fluxes within a few weeks. This was accompanied by a change in the width and strength of the Balmer lines and the slope of the blue continuum. However, based on the X-ray follow-up observations (XMM, Swift, Chandra) of these sources, we inferred that their X-ray properties are completely different. In this talk, I will present results from the X-ray spectral analysis and broadband SED fitting of these rare CLAGN found during transition. I will discuss the differences in their X-ray evolution as they undergo transition and how they can be used to shed light on the inner regions of AGN.

Neeraj Kumari INAF-IASF Palermo
Tracing accretion-state transitions in AGN: prospects for NewAthena
Abstract

Changing-look active galactic nuclei (CLAGN) provide a unique opportunity to investigate how supermassive black hole accretion flows evolve. Our long-term, multi-wavelength study of NGC 3822 revealed more than an order-of-magnitude variation in bolometric luminosity over 17 years, accompanied by the appearance and disappearance of broad optical emission lines. The association between the broad-line state and higher Eddington ratio supports an accretion-driven origin for this transition, although variable absorption may also contribute. Complementary broadband analysis of a sample of narrow-line Seyfert galaxies (NLS1s) reveals a weak positive correlation between X-ray photon-index and Eddington ratio, alongside coronal properties consistent with efficient Compton cooling. Together, these results motivate investigating how the X-ray corona responds to changes in accretion state.

NewAthena's high throughput and high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy will enable tighter constraints on coronal temperature, high-energy cutoff,absorption and disk reflection. Time-resolved observations could distinguish intrinsic changes in the accretion flow from variable obscuration and test whether coronal evolution accompanies the changing-look transition. This will help establish how the inner disk-corona system responds to changing accretion conditions and shapes the observable evolution of AGNs.

Changing state AGN in X-rays
Abstract

Accreting supermassive black holes are intrinsically variable, changing in brightness across the electromagnetic spectrum and over a wide range of timescales. In recent years, a remarkable subset of active galactic nuclei has been found to exhibit extreme spectral variability. These changing-state events are thought to be driven by rapid and dramatic changes in the black hole accretion rate, challenging the standard expectations for accretion timescales in systems hosting black holes with masses of millions to billions of solar masses. Such transitions provide a unique opportunity to probe the structure and evolution of accretion flows around massive black holes.

In my talk, I will present recent observational results on nearby changing-state AGN, highlighting how their X-ray emission evolves during the transitions, and what these changes reveal about the structure of the accretion flow. I will also discuss how the capabilities of Athena will open new avenues for studying rapid accretion-state transitions in AGN.

Guido Risaliti University of Florence, Italy
Quasars as Standard Candles with NewAthena
Abstract

The non-linear relation between X-ray and UV luminosity offers a powerful tool both to probe the disk–corona connection in quasars and to derive their distances, thereby turning quasars into high-redshift standard candles. Over the past decade, studies with XMM-Newton and Chandra have demonstrated that the main bottleneck lies on the X-ray side: the absence of a wide-field X-ray survey with depth comparable to the SDSS (for a typical quasar SED) has limited our ability to validate this method and to construct a quasar Hubble diagram comparable to that of supernovae. These limitations will be overcome with NewAthena. In its first three years of regular operations, NewAthena will deliver tens of thousands of serendipitous, high-quality X-ray spectra of optically selected quasars. This unprecedented dataset will enable precise measurements of cosmic expansion in the crucial redshift range z = 3–6, which remains poorly constrained by other major cosmological probes.

Guglielmo Mastroserio IUSS Pavia
Constraining the Hubble Constant with AGN Reverberation and NewAthena
Abstract

The Hubble constant (H0) quantifies the present rate of cosmic expansion, a fundamental parameter in modern cosmology. However, a significant discrepancy (approximately 5σ) exists between direct measurements using the traditional distance ladder method and indirect inferences based on cosmic microwave background observations. X-ray reverberation mapping of active galactic nuclei provides an independent route to constrain H0: by simultaneously modeling the time-averaged spectrum and the Fourier-resolved spectral–timing response, it is possible to constrain the intrinsic geometry and scaling of the accretion flow, effectively turning reverberating AGNs into standard candles. I will show that NewAthena, with its unprecedented combination of throughput and spectral resolution, will critically reduce degeneracies between reverberation model parameters and measure H0 with the precision required to arbitrate the current tension.

Alaa Salah Afifi Kepler Space University (USA)
Time-Domain Signatures and Cyclic Feedback of Supermassive Black Holes: Observations, Modeling and the EBBY/FLOW Framework
Abstract

Supermassive black holes (SMBHs) show very complicated changes over time due to variations in accretion, effects of relativity, and the behavior of plasma in magnetic fields. Multi-messenger astrophysics has now allowed us to explore such transient changes, which have been connected with features like quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs), tidal disruption events (TDEs), and bursts of jet activity, thanks to instruments like the Event Horizon Telescope, JWST, TESS, and future projects such as ATHENA. We describe in this chapter the EBBY/FLOW framework, which is essentially a physically inspired cyclic model that represents SMBH evolution through phases of energy storage (EBBY) alternating with energy release (FLOW). EBBY/FLOW synthesizes both the observational facts and the theoretical models of accretion disks, non-linear resonance, and magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) transport phenomena. These differential equations, which represent disk mass changes, resonant vibrations, and feedback through jets plus transient energy input, are used to unfold the model. Besides determining the scaling laws that relate the variability periods with black hole mass, we also put forward some observational signals that can be checked by the present time-domain surveys and upcoming ones. The EBBY/FLOW model not only offers a comprehensive understanding of the SMBH variability but also lays down a foundation for the integration of accretion physics with the major feedback mechanisms at large scales.

Valerio Pandolfi SRON - Space Research Organisation Netherlands
Machine Learning-Accelerated AGN Outflows Simulations for Density Estimation via Coherence Spectral Analysis
Abstract

Spectral-timing techniques offer a way to constrain the density of AGN outflows, and hence their location and energetics: as the ionizing continuum varies, the absorbing gas responds with density-dependent delays, which leave a characteristic imprint on the coherence-energy spectrum (Juráňová et al., 2022; Pandolfi et al., in prep.). Applying this method to warm absorbers requires resolving individual absorption features on variability timescales. NewAthena’s X-IFU will make this possible by combining high spectral resolution with a large effective area. We present simulations of X-IFU observations of variable AGN based on our time-dependent photoionization model of the absorbers, accelerated by neural network emulators. This machine learning-driven optimization reduces simulation runtimes by more than three orders of magnitude, enabling a systematic exploration of the parameter space, in turn, a robust assessment of how well coherence-spectral analysis can constrain the density of AGN outflows. Our results bring us closer to applying spectral-timing methods to observational data and highlight NewAthena's potential to advance our understanding of AGN feedback.

Accurate Time-Evolving diagnostic with NewAthena/X-IFU to constrain physics, location and energetics of the ionized environment of AGNs
Abstract

High-resolution X-ray spectroscopy probes the physical conditions and geometry of ionized gas around active galactic nuclei (AGN). I will present an application of TEPID2, a major upgrade of the Time-Evolving PhotoIonisation Device (TEPID; Luminari et al. 2023), now including an integrated level-population solver. Using multi-epoch XMM-Newton observations, we investigate the OVII soft X-ray emission lines in NGC 4051, a highly variable Seyfert galaxy hosting a multi-phase warm absorber, whose ionization responds to changes in the primary continuum. Due to the limited sensitivity of XMM/RGS, we obtain a consistent result but with limited significance, since an origin in more distant gas (such as the Narrow Line Region), where light-travel delays may smooth the response to continuum variations, cannot be fully ruled out. Alfredo Luminari INAF - Astronomical Observatory of Rome
Towards a fully self-consistent, time-evolving ionisation modelling: TEPID-2
Abstract

Variability is a hallmark of AGNs. Both the variability itself and the response of the surrounding matter to it are among the most powerful tools to probe AGN physics and composition. Photoionized gas is no exception to this: its variability can fully reveal its fundamental properties. Albeit long needed, time-evolving ionisation models are becoming available only recently. I will present a substantial update of the Time-Evolving PhotoIonisation Device, i.e. TEPID-2, which finally computes self-consistently the electron level populations and, thus, the gas emission spectrum. I will provide a quantitative overview of the capabilities of TEPID-2 in light of the expected performances of Athena/XIFU and of the XRISM/Resolve microcalorimeter, highlighting the unique diagnostics enabled by time-resolved absorption and emission spectroscopy.

Daniele Rogantini University of Chicago
How much energy do supermassive black hole winds carry? From Seyferts to nuclear transients with NewAthena
Abstract

AGN winds are considered one of the main channels of AGN feedback, as they can carry enough momentum and energy to regulate or quench star formation in their host galaxies. However, the energy carried by these winds is still poorly known, because it depends on the distance of the gas from the black hole, which cannot be derived from a single spectrum. When the ionising source varies, the gas responds with a delay that depends on its density. By modelling this delay with time-dependent photoionisation models, we can measure the location of the gas and therefore the momentum and energy of the wind. I will present an application of this method to Mrk 766, based on a coordinated XMM-Newton, NuSTAR, XRISM, Swift and NICER campaign, where the Swift and NICER monitoring was designed specifically for time-dependent modelling. Using our code TPHO, we model the response of the warm absorber and constrain the location and energetics of the outflow. I will then show how this approach will become key in the NewAthena era, when the X-IFU will allow us to measure the energetics of winds beyond the local Universe. Finally, I will show how time-dependent photoionisation can be applied to winds in nuclear transients, such as tidal disruption events and quasi-periodic eruptions. In these sources, the luminosity changes by large factors, which makes them good candidates to study the energetics of the wind.

Pierpaolo Condò Tor Vergata University of Rome
Mapping AGN Wind Geometry: From XRISM to NewAthena with RED-WINE
Abstract

The geometry of active galactic nucleus (AGN) winds remains a major uncertainty in determining their kinetic power and role in feedback. Combining absorption, which probes gas along our line of sight, with emission provides a route to reconstructing their global structure.

We present new results from XRISM observations of PDS 456 obtained with RED-WINE, an updated implementation of the Wind in the Ionized Nuclear Environment framework. The model combines photoionization calculations with fast analytical treatments of relativistic absorption and emission. Preliminary results suggest an angularly confined wind, covering approximately 40% of the sky as seen from the central source. However, the limited signal-to-noise of the emission features and degeneracies between gas properties and geometry leave important uncertainties.

NewAthena/X-IFU will transform this approach through its larger effective area and high spectral resolution extending into the soft X-ray band. Simulations demonstrate the potential to substantially improve constraints on wind geometry and ionization structure in roughly one third of the exposure time, accessing emission diagnostics beyond the Fe K complex. These capabilities will enable more informative studies of wind variability and energetics, strengthening the connection between nuclear wind measurements and tests of AGN feedback.

Laura Borrelli INAF-OAS, University of Bologna
NewAthena unprecedented view on extremely energetic UFOs in the local Universe
Abstract

Quasi-relativistic AGN nuclear winds, observed as highly ionized X-ray Ultra-Fast Outflows (UFOs), are among the most promising mechanisms powering AGN feedback and shaping SMBH/host-galaxy co-evolution. Assessing the relation between AGN power and the maximum UFO velocity is crucial to determine their still-debated launching mechanism. We present the first systematic XMM-Newton study of 33 bright sources searching for UFOs in the 7-12 keV band, leading to the detection of four previously unknown UFOs with v>0.3c, confirming that local Seyferts host such high-velocity winds and challenging our current knowledge about the linear v_out-L_Bol correlation. However, CCDs cannot resolve the velocity and ionization structure of these winds; we show how high-resolution spectroscopy fills this gap in one of them, NGC 7469, combining XMM-Newton/RGS and XRISM/Resolve data from our recent campaign to cover both the soft-band and the Fe-K region, revealing distinct atomic features due to a v>0.31c wind. This synergy anticipates NewAthena: we show simulations of X-IFU resolving the multi-zone wind across 0.2-12 keV in local Seyferts like NGC 7469 in only a few tens of ks, unveiling the clumpy UFO forest, testing acceleration mechanisms, and accurately determining the wind kinetic power in this almost unexplored velocity regime in the local Universe. Moreover, WFI+X-IFU will push such studies to higher redshift, crucial to determine the impact of AGN at different cosmic times.

Abstract

Active galactic nuclei exhibit variability across a wide range of spatial and temporal scales, reflecting the interplay between accretion and outflows. Abstract

Quasar-driven nuclear outflows, observed as highly ionized X-ray Ultra-Fast Outflows (UFOs) and UV Broad Absorption Lines (BALs), are crucial drivers of AGN feedback and galaxy co-evolution. Unveiling AGN Outflow Launching Mechanisms and Jet-Wind Connections with NewAthena/X-IFU
Abstract

NewAthena is designed to solve the long-standing mystery of how accreting black holes co-evolve with their host galaxies. Outflows launched by AGN, in the form of winds and jets, are promising mechanisms connecting super-massive black hole growth with galaxy evolution; however, their launching mechanisms, physical structure, and interplay remain poorly understood. Tracing Multiphase Gas Coupling and AGN Feedback in M87
Abstract

How active galactic nuclei (AGN) transfer energy to their surroundings and regulate gas cooling remains a central question in galaxy evolution. Owing to its proximity and prominent jet, M87 provides a unique laboratory to investigate these processes through the connection between its hot X-ray-emitting atmosphere and the warm ionized and cold molecular gas. Where cold molecular gas is detected, it closely follows the warm ionized gas in both position and velocity. Together, these results provide new insight into how local conditions and AGN activity shape the dynamics, excitation, and connections of multiphase gas in M87.

NewAthena and the Multi-Phase View of AGN Feedback at Cosmic Noon
Abstract

AGN-driven winds are key to shaping galaxy growth and are now detected across multiple gas phases, from X-ray to ionized and molecular outflows. AGN outflows: scaling relations and mass outflow rates
Abstract

Active galactic nuclei (AGN) can launch powerful galactic outflows that reshape the interstellar medium and regulate star formation. Relations between outflow properties and galaxy/AGN parameters can help us understand this process. However, these relations can depend on how outflowing gas is selected and how the mass outflow rate is calculated.

Chandra Bahadur Singh
Winds Versus Jets in Active Galactic Nuclei
Abstract

The anti-correlation between disk winds and relativistic jets in X-ray binaries is often interpreted as scale-invariant black hole accretion, implying a mass-scaled analog in active galactic nuclei (AGN). We test this across FRII radio quasars, radio-quiet quasars, and jetted and non-jetted Narrow Line Seyfert 1 galaxies (NLS1) in spirals, and find simple scale invariance is excluded. The highest-velocity winds occur exclusively in radio-quiet quasars, while powerful FRII quasars host systematically weaker winds despite equally large black hole masses. Jetted NLS1s show strong wind suppression like X-ray binaries, whereas FRII quasars occupy a distinct regime where jets and winds coexist. Black hole mass and spin magnitude alone cannot explain this dichotomy. We argue the critical parameter is the disk's angular momentum direction relative to the black hole's. Co-rotation, favored in secularly fueled spirals and most post-merger systems, brings compact ISCO radii, high radiative efficiency, strong winds, and jet suppression, while counter-rotation in a subset of merger-influenced ellipticals sustains powerful jets with moderate winds. Because AGN lack the rapid state transitions and magnetic flux redistribution of X-ray binaries, they are not strict scaled analogs, and at least two distinct wind–jet regimes operate across the mass scale.

Atsushi Tanimoto (Imagawa) Osaka Gakuin University
Constraints on the Torus Structure of the Circinus Galaxy with XRISM and IXPE
Abstract

The X-ray spectrum and polarization of the Circinus galaxy, a nearby Compton-thick active galactic nucleus, probe the obscuring torus independently: IXPE constrains the angular thickness of the reflector, and XRISM Resolve measures the width of the Fe Kα line. We model the torus with a Gaussian column-density profile of dispersion σ in the polar angle and gas in Keplerian rotation, and compute its X-ray spectrum and polarization with the Monte Carlo code MONACO. A torus with σ = 15°, an equatorial column density of 10^25 cm^-2, and inner and outer radii of 0.7 and 1.0 pc predicts a polarization degree of ≈33% for the scattered continuum at 2–6 keV, perpendicular to the symmetry axis, consistent with 28 ± 7% measured with IXPE. The polarization alone allows σ ≈ 11°–19° for equatorial column densities of 10^24.5–10^26 cm^-2; the line-of-sight column density of (6–10)×10^24 cm^-2 breaks the degeneracy and narrows the range to σ ≈ 12°–16° for 10^25–10^25.5 cm^-2. A thin torus with σ = 10° is disfavored at the 3σ level at the best-fit inclination. The same torus reproduces the XRISM spectrum at 2–8 keV and the NuSTAR spectrum up to 60 keV, including the Compton shoulder and hump, with a single reflection component at i ≈ 79°. If the Fe Kα width of 210 km/s is due to Keplerian rotation, the line-emitting material lies at 0.8 ± 0.2 pc, an order of magnitude outside the dust sublimation radius. Both probes require a geometrically thick torus at ~1 pc from the black hole.

Probing AGN Torus Structure Out to z~3: The First Census of Obscuration Variability with NewAthena
Abstract

Line-of-sight obscuration in AGN is not static, but rather varies on timescales from days to years, providing a direct probe of the clumpy structure of the torus, which can be used to place constraints on physical models. This will enable the first census of obscuration variability beyond the local Universe, testing clump distributions, torus geometry, and the evolution of torus density with cosmic time. Additionally, this will create a legacy sample of AGN for variability studies of all kinds, including luminosity and accretion rate, out to z~3.

Dust Reverberation Mapping of NGC 4151: The Dust Torus as the Origin of the Narrow Neutral Fe Kα Emission
Abstract

We carried out near-infrared monitoring observations of the nearby Seyfert 1 galaxy NGC 4151 simultaneously with XRISM X-ray observations to constrain the origin of the neutral Fe Kα emission.

Dust reverberation mapping provides a direct observational measure of the location of hot dust in the dust torus, which serves as a reference scale for investigating the Fe Kα emitting region. We monitored the target for two seasons (2023 and 2024) using two ground-based telescopes in Japan. The near-infrared light curves show significant variability, lagging behind the optical continuum variations by approximately 30 to 40 days.
